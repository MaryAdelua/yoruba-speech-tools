Conclusion: suitable for provisional, manually reviewed pitch-contour inspection
and later supervised alignment of stable voiced regions; NOT validated for fully
automatic natural-speech F0 acceptance, speech boundary detection or Yoruba scores.
No alignment, native-speaker comparison or linguistic scoring was performed.

Original assistant.wav remains unchanged. No trimming, denoising, normalization,

14.15% is pYIN's whole-clip voiced-frame percentage, not percentage of speech.
95/110 frames within the provisional region are pYIN-voiced (86.36%).

65 HZ AND PRE-SPEECH INVESTIGATION
Exactly 65 Hz occurs in 10 frames, 0.31–0.40 s. All have pYIN probability 0.01,
RMS about -72.4 to -73.9 dBFS, and no voiced Praat counterpart. Lowering the floor
to 50 Hz moves these estimates to about 50–53.3 Hz; raising it to 80 Hz makes
them unvoiced. This is strong evidence of low-level non-speech being assigned
floor-sensitive F0, not a stable 65 Hz vocal fundamental and not a Yoruba tone.
Other early detections, 0–0.08 s near 70 Hz, also have probability 0.01 and lack
Praat voicing. Noise/tracking artifacts are likely; start-edge padding may also
contribute at the beginning. Their physical source remains undetermined.
The 0.78–0.79 s detections are different: centered 85.33 ms windows overlap the
speech onset. They are consistent with window-related onset spread.
Every suspicious frame remains in comparison_frames.csv and flagged_frames.csv.

SETTINGS SENSITIVITY AND ESTIMATOR CHOICE
At 50 Hz floor, speech agreement remains similar (80 common frames, median
0.5353 Hz difference), but pre-speech false detections remain. At 80 Hz floor,
pYIN speech coverage falls from 95 to 50 frames. Raising the floor is not a
justified cleanup. Preserve the 65–500 Hz baseline and both estimates.
Praat appears more selective for this recording's background and more precise
on clean sines. pYIN has broader temporal support and boundary spread. These
observations justify using Praat as a cross-check, not declaring a universal winner.
All 95 pYIN-voiced speech frames have probability below 0.5. This diagnostic
threshold cannot be treated as a calibrated acceptance/rejection rule.

SYNTHETIC CHECKS
20/20 checks passed: 10 signals evaluated by two estimators.
Constant sines: 80, 100, 150, 220, 350 and 450 Hz, amplitude 0.2, 2 s at 24 kHz.
Other signals: digital silence; 150 Hz with amplitude 0.2 to 0.02; 100 to 220 Hz
step; 150 Hz burst at 0.8–1.2 s surrounded by silence. Exact WAVs and frame CSVs
are in synthetic/. Float WAV storage preserves the generated signal precision.
All steady voiced intervals have 100% coverage. Worst constant-sine p95 error:
pYIN 10.53 cents (0.1053 semitone), Praat 0.55 cents. No false voicing in steady
digital silence. Checks require >=95% coverage, <=50 cents p95 error, and zero
false voicing in steady silence. +/-100 ms around transitions and file edges
are excluded from these steady checks, not deleted from outputs.
Important boundary failures: pYIN's short burst spans frame centers 0.77–1.23 s
despite true support 0.8–1.2 s; Praat spans 0.81–1.19 s. At the F0 step, pYIN
has six unvoiced frames 0.98–1.03 s; Praat produces an intermediate 109.24 Hz at
1.0 s. Neither estimator should be treated as an exact boundary detector.
Clean-signal success does not establish robustness to real capture noise,
breathiness, creaky voice, or all speaker ranges. No such general claim is made.
The four original acoustic unit tests were rerun and passed. git diff --check
passed. Matplotlib's older unit-test plotting path fell back to temporary cache;
the new validation runner uses a writable local plotting cache.

NORMALIZATION
12*log2(F0 / estimator-specific full-clip voiced median).
pYIN baseline 99.0923 Hz; Praat baseline 100.4921 Hz. These are provisional and
content-dependent. Separate normalization may conceal systematic offsets, so
agreement statistics use raw-Hz ratios. No H/M/L labels are assigned.
